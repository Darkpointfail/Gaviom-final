Legitimate sweepstakes companies in the USA publish rules you can read before paying, offer free entry into the same pool, and never charge winners to claim prizes. Here is the compliance checklist discerning entrants—and brands—use in 2026.

Corporate identity and rules first

A legitimate operator names a US sponsor entity on every promotion. Gaviom Inc. (Delaware) links Official Rules site-wide, with eligibility, ARV, entry methods, and draw dates—not a PDF trapped behind an email gate.

If you cannot find the sponsor, the cap, or the closing date within two clicks, treat the site as unfinished or risky.

Free entry is non-negotiable for public sweepstakes

When paid entries exist, lawful US sweepstakes include Alternate Method of Entry with the same odds. Legitimate sweepstakes companies document AMOE on a dedicated page with handwriting requirements and mailing address.

Gaviom publishes free entry by mail instructions for each founding promotion ID. Operators that hide or obstruct AMOE fail the baseline test.

Odds, caps, and fulfillment proof

  • Capped pools with displayed odds—or clear material limits in rules
  • Prize specs photographed before entries open, not stock-photo vagueness
  • Reserved value or escrow messaging before the pool fills
  • Public draws or archived recordings of random selection
  • Winner process without upfront unlock fees

Gaviom combines these on consumer founding draws listed on active sweepstakes. For escrow detail, read prize escrow and trust.

Consumer platforms vs. B2B sweepstakes vendors

Some legitimate sweepstakes companies serve the public; others build private employee programs with closed eligibility. Do not mix those intents on one landing page—HR teams need different rules than nationwide AMOE promotions.

Consumer players should stay on public prize catalogs. Employers evaluating vendors should review Gaviom for Business separately for Perks and Event programs.

Scam patterns legitimate companies avoid

Winner fee requests, cryptocurrency unlocks, DMs-only notifications, and missing tax documentation processes are scam hallmarks. The FTC publishes consumer guidance on sweepstakes fraud—legitimate operators align with those red-flag lists.

Compare any company you discover against Gaviom's public stack: is Gaviom legit?, sweepstakes with displayed odds, and charitable giving policy.

Frequently asked questions

What makes a sweepstakes company legitimate?

Named US sponsor, published Official Rules, working free AMOE, real prize specs, no winner fees, and documented fulfillment or public draws.

Are aggregator sweepstakes sites legitimate companies?

Many aggregators list third-party links with outdated terms. Legitimate operators run their own promotions with a single sponsor entity.

Do legitimate companies publish odds?

Capped-pool operators should. Open-ended viral campaigns often cannot. Displayed odds are a strong legitimacy signal.

How is Gaviom structured as a sweepstakes company?

Gaviom Inc. (Delaware) operates US promotions with reserved prize value, live draws, AMOE, and published caps on founding pools.

When should a company use a sweepstakes vendor?

Brands running employee or consumer promotions need counsel-drafted rules, registration where required, and audit trails—see Gaviom for Business for B2B programs.

Rules, AMOE, caps, live draws, and reserved prize value on founding promotions.
